The story

In a city where buildings fold into portable parcels, their ground stays behind.

Ruth Vale, a removal inspector, is selling her inherited engine yard to buy a coastal motel. She has already assigned its front desk to her daughter Lena, a bus mechanic with a paid marine apprenticeship and a ferry ticket of her own.

Rushing her last clearance, Ruth signs Calder House empty without entering. She folds sixteen flats with thirty-two people still at home. Lena gets her to use the engine yard's certified pad. The building opens, everybody walks out, and cooperative chair Marta takes custody of its original master plan.

The residents have secure building storage and temporary rooms available. They want their standing home and their shared daily life. Ruth finds a thirty-day licence for a freight concourse, hoping to repair the harm while preserving her own sale. The cooperative agrees to one empty-building trial. Ruth understates a new obstruction in the moving stair's path. The south wall cracks and volunteer Nabil's wrist is injured. Marta ends Ruth's control of further moves; Lena discovers the false certificate.

Ruth asks the developer Bell to return their old pad. He refuses to change his development but keeps the paid storage and room bookings. Ruth reports both her failures. The residents choose the rooms and begin to leave.

Lena tells Ruth about the apprenticeship. Even without her daughter, Ruth can afford the motel and hire help. She chooses another use for her land: permanent title for the cooperative, with no reserved flat or authority. Marta puts the offer to the residents. Their temporary beds remain available.

Licensed operator Sana opens the damaged building while everyone waits outside. Lena shores the crooked wall, hands over to the crew and leaves for her ferry. Ruth works where Sana tells her to stand. The residents walk through an ordinary front door.

Weeks later, Ruth does paid maintenance for the cooperative. Her inspection licence is cancelled; fines and Nabil's treatment bills remain. Lena sends a video about a mistake at her new job. Ruth asks how the rest of her shift went. Above the yard's dinner table, the windows still sit crooked.
